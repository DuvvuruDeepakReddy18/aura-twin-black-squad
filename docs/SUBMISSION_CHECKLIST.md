1. Follow the root README's launch and verification instructions on a fresh local copy.
2. Open the core PDFs and v2 supplement and inspect every page. Ensure original metrics match `data/metrics.json` and stress claims match `data/stress.json`; do not combine the cohorts or label the stress audit independent clinical validation.
3. Confirm the folder name uses the team's real name and college. GitHub's repository slug may use hyphens; preserve the required team/college naming in the submitted project folder or root description.
4. Inspect the staged files. Exclude credentials, environment secrets, package caches, virtual environments, private contacts, and local scratch work.
5. Review the MIT license attribution. Third-party dependencies retain their own licenses; the project license does not replace them.
6. Make the repository public. Do not assume a private invite or signed-in view satisfies the evaluator's access requirement.
7. Verify the included v1 `AURA_Twin_Walkthrough.mp4` plays completely and lasts at least 20 minutes. It uses synthesized narration and actual prototype screenshots. Include a clearly labeled v2 supplement or make a human-led replacement using `DEMO_RUNBOOK.md` and `JURY_CHALLENGE_SCRIPT.md`. Upload the chosen video(s), then paste actual URLs and durations into the README. A script, PDF, or placeholder link is not a completed video.
8. Test every required link in a signed-out/private browser. Download the PDFs from the public repository and play the video.
9. Run the Challenge Lab on the submitted version, export a receipt and verify it offline. Include failed-window behavior and the separate stress audit in the team rehearsal. A successful verifier check is local consistency, not authentication or clinical certification.
