# AURA Twin — technical jury Q&A

## What is the digital twin here?

A patient-specific computational state combines a fixed synthetic historical profile with a time-indexed wearable state. At each replay timestamp, the system updates the features and produces a forecast and risk estimate. This is a deliberately narrow, data-driven digital-twin proof of concept; it is not a mechanistic simulation of a whole human body.

## What exactly is predicted?

Numerical glucose at +30, +60, +90, and +120 minutes, plus the probability that the reading at **exactly +120 minutes is ≥180 mg/dL**. This is not “any high reading in the next two hours,” and it does not predict every type of adverse event. The binary boundary is a prototype convention informed by the common 70–180 mg/dL context, not an individualized clinical target.

## Is the data real?

No. Both streams are generated by a seeded simulator in this repository. Patient identifiers, histories, and wearable traces are wholly synthetic. We do not claim results on Synthea, MIMIC-IV, hospital data, Apple Health, Google Fit, or a medical IoT connection. Synthea is a possible future integration, not the present data source.

## Why not a deep neural network?

For this proof of concept, an inspectable model is easier to reproduce, export, test, and explain. Model complexity is not evidence of clinical utility. Ridge regression provides numerical forecasts; logistic classification supplies event risk. Future model choices should be justified by independent validation, calibration, robustness, and clinician usefulness rather than novelty alone.

## Does fusion actually help?

Read the final ablation results in `data/metrics.json` and the Model evidence UI. Compare fused, sensor-only, static-only, and persistence baselines on the same held-out patients. Report both strengths and failures. Because the simulator includes relationships between static context and glucose kinetics, any fusion advantage is conditional on that synthetic design. It is not proof of benefit on real patients.

## How do you prevent leakage?

Split by patient before fitting. Fit standardization on training patients only. Construct features from data at or before the prediction timestamp. Use future readings only as labels. Use validation patients for calibration/interval estimation and reserve the test patients for final reporting. Repeated windows within one patient are correlated; a large window count is not the same as that many independent people.

## What do the intervals mean?

They use validation residuals to describe observed forecast error under the simulator. They do not establish clinical reliability, patient-conditional coverage, or guaranteed coverage after a distribution shift. Review the reported held-out coverage alongside interval width and point accuracy.

## Are the driver explanations causal?

No. A linear/logistic contribution describes the fitted model's mathematical dependence on its features. Correlated inputs and explicit interactions complicate interpretation. A positive log-odds contribution is not a probability-point change, a diagnosis, or evidence of a causal mechanism.

## Does the what-if tool tell a doctor which intervention will work?

No. It is a hypothetical sensitivity display, with simplified assumptions. It has not been trained or evaluated to estimate causal treatment effects. It cannot support medication changes, dosing, or individualized recommendations.

## What happens if a sensor is missing, late, or wrong?

The prototype forward-fills continuous vital measurements and uses zero for missing step/sleep increments. It abstains from risk and forecast output with less than 24 hours of history, more than 20% missingness in the checked recent windows, glucose older than 15 minutes, or heart rate/HRV older than 60 minutes. Inspect `data/model.json` and the inference code for the exact gate. These rules are development guardrails; the imputation assumptions and thresholds have not been validated on medical devices. Real deployments still need device-specific evaluation, monitoring and failure-response ownership.

## Why does the dashboard have alerts if it is not clinically validated?

Alerts demonstrate a review workflow around a configured model threshold. Acknowledgement records a local demo action; it does not confirm safety or create a clinical escalation. A deployment would need an evaluated threshold, an escalation owner, measured alert burden, governance, and a reliable audit trail.

## Is it compliant with HIPAA or India's DPDP Act?

We do not claim legal compliance or certification. The prototype uses generated data and does not ingest real patient records. A real deployment requires a separate legal, security, privacy, clinical, and regulatory review appropriate to its jurisdiction and intended use.

## What has actually been implemented?

Reproducible synthetic data generation and model training, exported model artifacts, held-out evaluation with ablations, a local inference path, and an interactive clinician dashboard with patient context, forecasts, replay, model evidence, hypothetical scenarios, and local review/export interactions. Inspect the README and source for the precise final feature set.

## What is future work?

Real device/EHR integration, independent clinical data validation, broader stress testing, secure authentication, access controls, governed audit logs, multi-user clinical records, prospective studies, deployment monitoring, and any regulated clinical use. No outcome improvement, time saving, or financial benefit is claimed as measured today.

## Why would this project be useful to a jury?

It makes the full chain inspectable: provenance → time alignment → feature fusion → model → held-out evidence → user workflow. It is a working proof of concept with explicit failure boundaries and reproducible artifacts, rather than an untestable concept slide.
