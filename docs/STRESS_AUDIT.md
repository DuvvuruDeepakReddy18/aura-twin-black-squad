Generated by `python ml/stress_test.py`. All results below are synthetic. The trained model was not changed.

Frozen coefficients, scaling, calibration, intervals and alert threshold. Same held-out patient identities; all eligible 30-minute-stride windows. Only main-meal timing is altered; snack, activity and noise draw sequences remain unchanged.

Each case uses **48 held-out synthetic patients and 6,720 correlated windows**. Target: glucose >=180 mg/dL at exactly +120 minutes. Confidence intervals and all horizons are in [`data/stress.json`](../data/stress.json).

## Interpretation

Regular meal timing makes this simulator easier to predict. Delaying or varying meals challenges the clock features and can sharply reduce interval coverage and event recall. A good score on the original generator does not establish robustness. The product exposes this limitation rather than silently treating its interval as a safety guarantee.

## Protocol and limits

- Synthetic sensitivity audit, not external or clinical validation.
- These stress cases were specified after examining the model's clock features; this is a transparent post-hoc audit, not a preregistered benchmark.
- The same test patients recur across cases, so comparisons are paired and observations are not independent.
- The model is not retrained on stress cases. No automatic drift detector is claimed.
- A nominal 90% interval is not guaranteed to retain coverage under distribution shift.
- Missing-sensor handling is exercised separately in Challenge lab; plausible CGM bias is a known undetected failure mode.
